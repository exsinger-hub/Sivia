SCIENTIFIC ARGUMENT AND PICTURE SCOPE

The reader should understand that a video is encoded once into a global scene representation F, and a shared lightweight decoder answers independent point queries. A query identifies a two-dimensional source location, the time at which that point is identified, the time at which its three-dimensional position is requested, and the camera coordinate system in which to express that position. These last three time indices have different meanings. They need not be equal. The method does not require separate learned decoders for depth, tracking and camera estimation. Make the query interface the central explanatory object, and make the dynamic three-dimensional scene the largest concrete visual object.

The source-supported mathematical interfaces are F = E(V), q = (u,v,t_src,t_tgt,t_cam), and P = D(q,F). The output P is a three-dimensional position. The source-video local patch centered at (u,v) supplements the coordinate and time embeddings when constructing the query token. The encoder uses local frame-wise and global self-attention. The decoder uses cross-attention from each query to F. Queries do not attend to one another. Once the video features have been computed, F remains fixed during this decoding stage; this is reuse of the scene representation, not a statement that the encoder weights are frozen during training.
